01 / THE PROBLEM

The dirty secret of AI consulting

Getting automation built is the easy part. The industry's real failure happens six months later, when the person who built it is gone and you own a system nobody understands.

failure-mode/1The Black Box

The agency shipped something impressive and left. Now a vendor update breaks it, nobody knows where the credentials live, and "just fix it" turns out to mean "rebuild it."

failure-mode/2The Hero Dependency

One person knows how the system actually works: which queue to check, which customer is mapped by hand, what happens when it jams. When they take a vacation, the backlog starts. When they leave, it's a crisis.

failure-mode/3The Missing Why

The code says what the system does. Nothing says why: which gaps are deliberate, which threshold was a promise to your VP, which shortcut was a trade-off. So the next engineer fixes things that were never broken.

02 / THE PRACTICE

Forward-deployed, built to hand over

I work the way forward-deployed engineers work: inside your business, on your systems, with your people. The difference is that everything I learn gets written down in a form the next person can run with.

1

Embed

I sit with the people who do the work and learn how your business actually runs: the systems, the vocabulary, who decides what, and where the real bottleneck is.

2

Ship

Working automation on your operating rhythm. When something errors it skips the item and reports it, rather than half-finishing. Human judgment stays where you want it, as a designed part of the system.

3

Document the why

Every deployment carries a manifest: the decisions with the alternatives we rejected, the constraints and who set them, the runbook for when things break, and what each term means in your building.

4

Hand over

Every human step has a named owner, a backup, and a walkthrough. Any competent engineer, human or AI, can maintain the system from the briefing. You are never hostage to whoever built it, and that includes me.

03 / THE METHOD

The method is published

The handover discipline is an open specification with working tooling, developed in public. Read it, audit it, or hold me to it.

spec/deployment-manifestThe Deployment Manifest

A machine-readable record of your environment and everything deployed into it: systems, data quirks, decisions with rejected alternatives, constraints, runbooks, and risks. It is written to be read cold by someone who wasn't there. 10 sections, validated in CI

spec/lineage + walkthroughsStaffing as a System

Every flow records its family tree: who has held each role, who holds it now, and the walkthrough that moves it to the next person. A staffing change becomes a procedure instead of a crisis, and the tooling flags single points of failure automatically. 0 unstaffed roles allowed

tools/brief.jsAgent-Ready by Design

The manifest compiles into a briefing an AI coding agent can maintain the system from. Your constraints become its hard rules and your approval boundaries become its stop signs, so maintenance stops depending on any one person's memory. 1 command to compile
