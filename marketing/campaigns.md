# Campaign briefs

## Campaign 1 — Last Real Item

**Purpose:** turn existing trust into process walkthroughs without pretending every
contact already needs an agent.

**Audience:** former clients, collaborators, vendors, agency owners, technical peers, and
founders who already know Brandon's work.

**Trigger:** a firsthand observation about a recurring handoff, exception, intake queue,
or project process. When no observation exists, lead with the relationship and ask about
the last item that stalled.

**Promise:** “Show me the last real item. I will tell you whether it needs a prompt, a
built-in feature, ordinary automation, an existing product, or an agent job.”

**Distribution:** personal email, LinkedIn DM, text, or an existing private community.
Choose the channel already normal for the relationship.

**Sequence:**

1. Day 0: personal note with one process question.
2. Day 5 or later: one concrete follow-up.
3. Stop after a reply, decline, or second silence. Do not add warm relationships to an
   automated sequence.

**Conversion path:** reply → 30-minute process walkthrough → $500 Map when the opportunity
is real but the scope is uncertain → $3,500 Pilot when value and boundaries are already
clear.

**Success threshold:** five walkthroughs from twenty approved contacts. If fewer than
three people reply, inspect the contact selection and opening observation before changing
the offer.

## Campaign 2 — The Dropped Handoff

**Purpose:** use the completed demo to make the difference between inference and an
installed job tangible.

**Audience:** owners and operations leaders at 5-to-50-person B2B service, professional,
logistics, and technical businesses where a won deal must move into billing, delivery,
and scheduling.

**Public signals:** a visible CRM/project stack, recent growth, hiring for operations or
project coordination, a documented onboarding process, or customer reviews that mention
handoff delays. A signal justifies a question; it does not prove a pain point.

**Distribution:** individually researched email, LinkedIn message after a relevant post,
and useful comments in operator communities. Apollo may supply accounts after connection,
but each first wave message stays handwritten.

**Asset:**
`https://0tomait.com/agent-job.html?utm_source=direct_email&utm_medium=outbound&utm_campaign=demo_handoff&utm_content=handwritten`

**Sequence:**

1. Day 0: cite the observable signal and ask where the last handoff stalled.
2. Day 4–6: send the demo only if it clarifies the question.
3. Day 10–12: close the loop with a simple opt-out.

**Success threshold:** two walkthroughs per fifty well-researched contacts. Do not scale
volume until a ten-contact sample produces at least one substantive reply or clear buyer
language about why it missed.

## Campaign 3 — AI or Not?

**Purpose:** restore the original advisory pitch while keeping it attached to the same
engagement path.

**Audience:** owners who know they should use AI but cannot tell a prompt, automation,
software feature, and agent job apart.

**Format:** one real-looking but composite business process per post. Classify it publicly
using five choices:

1. Prompt.
2. Built-in feature.
3. Ordinary automation.
4. Existing product.
5. Agent job.

Explain the disqualifier as clearly as the opportunity. End with one question: “What was
the last real item?”

**Distribution:** Brandon's LinkedIn profile first; reuse as a short email to existing
contacts and, after four strong editions, as a LinkedIn newsletter called **The Job After
the Prompt**.

**Cadence:** one short classification post and one deeper teardown each week for six
weeks. The deeper piece can become the newsletter only after the short posts show which
workflows earn replies.

**Conversion path:** post or newsletter → homepage → fit call or paid AI Leverage Map.

**Success threshold:** three relevant comments or direct replies across the first four
posts, and at least one process walkthrough attributable to the series.

## Campaign 4 — Automation Survival Audit

**Purpose:** attract teams that already have automations but are exposed to black boxes,
silent failures, missing ownership, and fragile handoffs.

**Audience:** founders, operations leads, MSP clients, agency clients, and businesses with
Zapier, Make, n8n, spreadsheet, or custom integrations already in production.

**Distribution:** LinkedIn image post, a five-minute live walkthrough in operator groups,
partner newsletters, meetup follow-ups, and one-to-one sharing when maintenance risk is
already part of the conversation.

**Asset:**
`https://0tomait.com/win.html?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=survival_audit&utm_content=score_post`

**CTA:** complete the ten questions, then bring the score and the weakest “no” to a call.
Do not gate the audit behind an email form in the first month.

**Success threshold:** fifty qualified visits, ten people who report or discuss a score,
and two walkthroughs. If the audit gets visits but no conversations, change the result CTA
before changing the questions.

## Campaign 5 — Operator Partners

**Purpose:** earn introductions from people who already see cross-system operational pain
but do not want to build custom agent systems themselves.

**Partner profiles:** fractional COOs, bookkeepers and fractional CFOs, RevOps consultants,
MSPs, IT consultants, CRM implementers, software agencies, and compliance consultants.

**Offer to the partner:** a no-pressure classification of one client process, a useful
demo they can share, and clear respect for simpler tools when they are enough. No referral
fee is promised until the economics and professional obligations are understood.

**Distribution:** ten personal partner notes, two small co-hosted process teardown sessions,
and a one-page partner brief. Never scrape or mass-message professional partners.

**Conversion path:** partner conversation → one client introduction → joint process
walkthrough → Map or Pilot.

**Success threshold:** three active partners and two qualified introductions per month by
day 90.

## Channel priorities

1. Warm direct outreach: fastest learning and highest trust.
2. Introductions and partners: borrowed trust with good problem visibility.
3. LinkedIn founder content: public proof and message testing.
4. Targeted outbound: controlled expansion once language is working.
5. SEO: compounding support, not a first-month demand source.
6. Paid ads: not before a page and message convert warm or targeted traffic.
